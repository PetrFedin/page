Partnership and investment

Partnership and investment in my own IT products and joint projects — in fashion and beyond.

Some products already run as prototypes and MVPs; others are still in development and need to be brought to delivery. The scope is not limited to fashion: I apply the same principles in other industries. I am looking for partners and investors to turn these products and ideas into joint business projects: finish what is missing, test it in practice, bring it to market. I discuss terms in person and promise no returns.

01

Equity investment

Capital for the next stage of a product

You take a stake in one of the products or in a new idea. The money goes to the pilot, further development and market entry, following a plan we agree.

02

Strategic partner

Your business as the first proving ground

A brand, retailer or company becomes the pilot customer. You work in the product on real tasks, and those tasks decide what I build first.

03

Co-development

Parts of one journey, not rivals

I connect my product to yours: shared data, a single user journey, or a module that fills a gap in the other. It is a separate project with clear boundaries.

04

Marketing and distribution

Your channel, my product

You take the product to your own audience through a client base, a community, media or an implementation team. I look after the product and make sure it works for your customers.

05

Events and sponsorship

A platform for your event

A platform for fashion weeks, forums and conferences: programme, registration, meetings and partner accounts. It has not yet been used at a real event, so I am looking for a first organiser. A sponsor or an organiser takes part in the launch.

06

Acquiring a product or stake

A product under your management

You buy the whole product or a share in it. I will consider a partial handover as readily as a full one, if you are ready to take the product forward.

    Syntha now runs deals across multiple legal entities

    Syntha now runs deals across multiple legal entities. In fashion wholesale, a brand, a distributor and a production partner almost never operate through one legal entity, especially when foreign counterparties are in the chain. What changed: Syntha has a legal-entity registry. You can set up Russian and foreign details and run a deal under the right entity, instead of one shared company profile. What it gives you: • A brand with several legal entities runs deals under each of them. • A partner in another jurisdiction works in the same system as everyone else. • Details of Russian and foreign legal entities are kept in one registry.

  2. 1 October 2026

    In Renova, an enquiry becomes a project in a single step

    In Renova, an enquiry becomes a project in a single step. Until now a renovation lead and a working project were only loosely connected: turning an enquiry into a project with a contractor, a client and a schedule meant checking by hand who was who. What changed: Managing project participants and converting a lead into active work now happen as a single operation. It has been tested under simultaneous use: two sessions at once do not drift apart in the data. The core mechanism is built and tested; completing the independent-contractor flow is in progress. What it gives you: • An enquiry becomes a project without manual checking. • Records of who runs a site no longer get lost at the seam between lead and project. • Two people can work on one project at once without conflicts.

    ChatX now has a company wiki

    ChatX now has a company wiki. Knowledge is usually kept everywhere except the messenger: pinned messages, files on a drive, in the heads of the longest-serving staff. What changed: A shared wiki – pages the whole team can edit, with full edit history. You can see who changed what and roll back to an earlier version. What it gives you: • A decision or process discussed in a channel is kept alongside it, not in a separate service. • Knowledge is not tied to one person. • Any edit can be rolled back.
