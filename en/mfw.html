01 — Diagnosis

Two events, each with its own PDFs, chats and mailings

Parallel events of this scale have no shared entry point: not for the guest, not for the brand, not for the industry.

  1. Scattered programmes

    Each event has its own schedule, participant list and navigation. PDFs, chats and mailings go out of date before they are published.

  2. Two sources instead of one

    A guest interested in both events has to cross-check two schedules and two sets of materials.

  3. A brand loses its audience right after the show

    The followers and loyalty a brand gains at a show do not carry over. At the next event it has to win the same audience again.

  4. No overall picture of the industry

    Organisers have no shared showcase or common figures to present the two events as linked parts of one industry.

02 — Approach

One stage, two events side by side

MFW puts two events in one space and gives each its own interface.

Shared: account, programme, notifications, loyalty, Brand CRM/CDP and analytics. A one-off show becomes a lasting link with the audience. Registration and pass are separate for each event, because the audience and the journey differ. MFW guides the visitor: Brand → Designer → Show → Collection → LIVE/Replay → Follow → Reward. BFS guides the delegate: Organisation → Speaker/Delegate → Session → Topic → Meeting → Follow → Lead.

The audience engages weeks before opening: guests follow brands and designers and build a personal list of shows and sessions in advance. The organiser prepares in the system rather than in spreadsheets and email threads. Each new season adds to the data and needs no rebuild.

03 — Who it is for

Guests, brands and organisers: a layer for each

One platform, a separate interface for each role: a simple route to the show for the guest, a lasting audience for the brand, industry-wide figures for the organiser.

  1. Participants and guests

    One paperless, queue-free journey: accreditation and QR, a programme with reminders, a directory of designers, brands, speakers and organisations, follows and favourites with a personal schedule, LIVE/Replay, buyer–delegate introductions and B2B meetings.

  2. Brands

    The audience stays after the show. The Brand 365 profile keeps working once the event is over: followers and favourites, verified 30-day loyalty, a rewards wallet with a one-time QR for the market and the showroom, news and photos, audience invitations, order import from the till and sales tracking.

  3. Organisers and owner

    A complete picture of the industry: shared registration for both events, synchronised official programme data, Brand CRM/CDP for all brands, and joined-up analytics on engagement and attendance across MFW and BFS.

04 — Scope

From the event programme to the owner’s analytics

The platform rests on three layers: participant experience, brand commerce and owner analytics. All three draw on the same data.

  1. Brand CRM/CDP

    A brand sees its audience as it would in its own online shop: segments (subscription age, 30+/60+, favourites, buyers), saved and overlapping segments, a campaign builder and scheduler that checks consent and frequency caps, and deterministic test and control groups.

  2. Commercial analytics

    The whole chain in numbers, from first click to repeat purchase: CAC/ROI, purchase attribution, average basket, repeat purchases, LTV, cohort retention, RFM, churn scoring, predicted CLV, next-best-action recommendations, acquisition-source economics, and automated journeys with branching and control groups.

  3. Owner Control Tower

    One dashboard instead of two reports for MFW and BFS: each brand’s contribution, M1/M3/M6 cohort retention, GMV by attribution and by uplift, predicted CLV, audience flow and overlap, and the mix of acquisition channels. Metrics are classified as observed, attributed, incremental or modelled. Modelled estimates are not presented as audited.
